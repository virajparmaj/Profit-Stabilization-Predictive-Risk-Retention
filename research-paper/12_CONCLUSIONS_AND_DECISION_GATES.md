## What can be concluded now

The project supports retrospective classification and descriptive expenditure analysis. Its cached high-score cohorts have lower mean spending and lower dollar SD but higher CV. Weighting changes the reported AUROC, and selected capacity differs when measured by population weight. Several code and construct issues prevent the existing “minimum stable model” and profit-stabilization claims from being sustained.

A plausible modest research contribution is available, but it has not been demonstrated: determine whether accounting for membership reproducibility and expenditure distributions materially changes conclusions from conventional classifier evaluation. Prior work already establishes the individual concepts.

## Evidence gates

| Gate | Pass condition | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| G0: valid data/constructs | Corrected definitions, cohort, weights and leakage checks pass | Repair before substantive model claims |
| G1: trustworthy score comparison | Source-trained paired scores with calibration and provenance | Do not use historical caches as corrected evidence |
| G2: meaningful identity result | E2 quantifies reproducibility at matched capacity with uncertainty and practical interpretation | Report stability/indistinguishability; do not invent hidden instability |
| G3: interpretable spending result | E3 distinguishes dollar from relative variability and survives zero-mass/control checks | Narrow claim to the actual decomposition or null result |
| G4: robustness | Main comparison survives reasonable E4 choices or its failure boundary is clearly explained | Report sensitivity; avoid generality |
| G5: independent support | Frozen E5 reproduces effect or explains a measured shift | Limit result to original population/year; investigate without tuning away failure |
| G6: reproducible manuscript | Each claim traces to a run and limitations match design | Resolve evidence gaps before calling it a finished study |

Passing a gate does not mean obtaining p<0.05. Precision, practical relevance, construct validity and transparent negative results matter. A discrepancy in one arbitrary metric is insufficient by itself.

## Conditional conclusions for possible outcomes

**Accuracy and stability disagree:** report the exact paired contrast, capacity, effect size, uncertainty and expenditure consequence. Limit the claim to the evaluated populations and model specifications. This is the preferred empirical contribution if it survives E4/E5.

**Accuracy and stability agree:** the central hypothesis is unsupported. Describe a validation result and assess whether the result materially qualifies prior expectations. Independent replication may strengthen a useful negative finding, but do not announce a novel mechanism.

**CV increase disappears after zero-mass standardization:** attribute the original pattern mainly to zero expenditure where supported. Do not describe higher CV as evidence of larger dollar tail risk. Standard decomposition is explanatory evidence, not a theorem contribution.

**Corrected variables reverse the conclusion:** the research becomes a transparent correction/robustness study. Preserve the historical result and explain which decisions caused the change. Do not conceal it by selecting a preferred preprocessing variant.

**Simple baselines match sophisticated models:** report this with justified noninferiority margins and paired intervals if supported. A tiny point-estimate difference does not prove sufficiency.

**Only one year or cutoff supports the finding:** report the boundary. The conclusion is context-specific, and the title/abstract must reflect that scope.

## Submission readiness today

**Not ready as a completed empirical paper claiming stable membership, prospective risk or financial benefit.** Ready as a research plan backed by a repository audit and exploratory numeric rechecks. Minimum next work: E0 corrections, E1 paired comparison, E2 refitting, E3 distribution evaluation and E4 robustness. Add E5 before making a strong generalization argument; E7 is required for forward-looking prediction claims.

The recommended title is “Evaluating Low-Spending Healthcare Cohorts: Membership Stability and Expenditure Dispersion in MEPS.” It accurately describes the proposed investigation without promising a favorable finding.

## Claim boundaries

No claims of causality, equivalent clinical need, prospective accuracy, intervention benefit, insurer profit, retention impact, optimal deployment or universally superior model are currently supported. “Stable” must name the estimand: selected identities, predicted probabilities, cohort size or expenditure variability. These are different properties.
